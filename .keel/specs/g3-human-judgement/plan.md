# Design — g3-human-judgement

## Approach

The seam is G3 in `src/gate/g3.rs`, which gains a `G3/human-oracles` check
beside its four existing ones. The check reads the judgements recorded for the
spec and compares each one's hash with the current run's diff and
`oracles.json`; a human oracle with no matching judgement, a superseded one or
a `fail` fails the check, and a `fail` also records a failure episode for G4.

Judgements are written, not inferred. `src/approval.rs` appends each one to
the spec's approval log and `src/evidence/**` chains it into the run's
evidence, bound to those hashes. A new command module, `src/cmd/review.rs`,
records them (`keel judge <slug> <criterion> --pass|--fail`) and writes the
review brief (`keel review <slug>`). It is wired in through `src/cmd/mod.rs`
and `src/main.rs`. The brief's hash is recorded by the later `merge` approval
in `src/cmd/approve.rs`. `src/metrics.rs` reads the brief and approval
timestamps to flag fast approvals, stating that it measures elapsed time and
not attention.

Naming collision to keep straight: `keel approve --stage review` approvals
already exist and are a different thing. They are a stage approval of the plan
and tasks. The new `keel review` command prints and records a brief for the
merge decision, and must not read, write or reuse the `review` stage in the
approval log.

Tasks are chained because they share `tests/g3_judgement.rs`,
`src/approval.rs`, `src/gate/g3.rs` and `src/evidence/**`; T-1 lands the
`G3/human-oracles` check and the rest build on it.

Risk: the task budgets total 440 of the 450 lines allowed, leaving 10 lines of
slack. T-2 touches seven files (a new command, the evidence chain and the CLI
wiring) and T-5 is the next largest; either could overrun. If one does, widen
the spec's budget deliberately rather than trimming tests.
